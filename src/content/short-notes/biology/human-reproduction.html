2.0 Reproductive events in humans

Gametogenesis→Insemination→Fertilisation→Implantation→Gestation→Parturition
  • Humans are sexually reproducing and viviparous. Reproductive events occur after puberty.
  • Remarkable differences between male and female: sperm formation continues even in old men, but formation of ovum ceases around the age of fifty in women.

2.1 The male reproductive system

Located in the pelvis region: a pair of testes, accessory ducts, glands and external genitalia.

Accessory ducts (sperm route)

Seminiferous tubules→Rete testis→Vasa efferentia→Epididymis→Vas deferens→Ejaculatory duct→Urethra→Urethral meatus
  • Epididymis lies along the posterior surface of each testis. Vas deferens ascends to the abdomen, loops over the urinary bladder, receives a duct from the seminal vesicle and opens into the urethra as the ejaculatory duct.
  • Penis: special tissue helps erection for insemination; enlarged end = glans penis, covered by the foreskin.

Male accessory glands

  • Paired seminal vesicles, a single prostate, paired bulbourethral glands.
  • Their secretions form seminal plasma, rich in fructose, calcium and certain enzymes. Bulbourethral secretions also help lubricate the penis.
TrapSertoli cells = nutrition (inside tubule); Leydig cells = androgens (outside tubule, interstitial). Prostate is single; seminal vesicles and bulbourethral glands are paired.

2.2 The female reproductive system

A pair of ovaries, a pair of oviducts, uterus, cervix, vagina and external genitalia in the pelvic region, plus a pair of mammary glands.

External genitalia

  • Mons pubis (cushion of fatty tissue with skin and pubic hair), labia majora (fleshy folds around vaginal opening), labia minora (paired folds under labia majora), hymen (membrane partially covering vaginal opening), clitoris (tiny finger-like structure at the upper junction of the labia minora, above the urethral opening).
  • The hymen may be torn by a fall, tampon or sports (horseback riding, cycling) and may persist after coitus — its presence or absence is not a reliable indicator of virginity or sexual experience.

Mammary glands

Alveoli (secrete milk)→Mammary tubules→Mammary duct→Mammary ampulla→Lactiferous duct

Glandular tissue of each breast has 15–20 mammary lobes containing clusters of cells called alveoli, which secrete milk stored in their lumens.

Memory trickOviduct from ovary to uterus: “I Am In” — Infundibulum, Ampulla, Isthmus. Fertilisation happens in the Ampulla.

2.3 Gametogenesis: spermatogenesis

  1. Begins at puberty. Spermatogonia on the inside wall of seminiferous tubules multiply by mitosis; each is diploid, 46 chromosomes.
  2. Some spermatogonia = primary spermatocytes; complete meiosis I (reduction division) → two equal, haploid secondary spermatocytes (23 chromosomes each).
  3. Meiosis II → four equal, haploid spermatids.
  4. Spermatids → spermatozoa by spermiogenesis. Sperm heads embed in Sertoli cells and are released from seminiferous tubules by spermiation.

2.3 Gametogenesis: oogenesis

  1. Initiated during embryonic development: a couple of million oogonia form in each foetal ovary; no more oogonia are formed after birth.
  2. Oogonia enter prophase I of meiosis and are temporarily arrested → primary oocytes.
  3. Primary oocyte + a layer of granulosa cells = primary follicle. Many degenerate from birth to puberty; at puberty only 60,000–80,000 primary follicles remain in each ovary.
  4. More granulosa layers + new theca = secondary follicle.
  5. Tertiary follicle: fluid-filled cavity called antrum; theca organised into theca interna and theca externa.
  6. Primary oocyte within the tertiary follicle completes meiosis I — unequal division → large haploid secondary oocyte (keeps the nutrient-rich cytoplasm) + tiny first polar body.
  7. Tertiary follicle → mature Graafian follicle; secondary oocyte forms a new membrane, the zona pellucida.
  8. Graafian follicle ruptures to release the secondary oocyte (ovum) = ovulation.

2.4 Menstrual cycle

  • Reproductive cycle of female primates (monkeys, apes, humans). First menstruation at puberty = menarche. Average interval about 28/29 days; one ovum released in the middle of each cycle.
PhaseOvaryUterus / hormones
Menstrual phase (3–5 days)—Breakdown of endometrial lining and its blood vessels → menstrual flow. Occurs only if the ovum is not fertilised. Lack of menstruation may indicate pregnancy (or stress, poor health).
Follicular phasePrimary follicles grow into a mature Graafian follicleEndometrium regenerates by proliferation; LH and FSH rise gradually, stimulating follicle development and estrogen secretion
Ovulatory phase (~14th day)Graafian follicle ruptures → ovulationLH and FSH peak; rapid LH rise = LH surge, which induces ovulation
Luteal phaseRemaining follicle → corpus luteumCorpus luteum secretes large amounts of progesterone to maintain the endometrium for implantation. No fertilisation → corpus luteum degenerates → endometrium disintegrates → menstruation.
NEET HighlightDuring pregnancy all events of the menstrual cycle stop. Cycles cease around 50 years = menopause. Cyclic menstruation indicates the normal reproductive phase between menarche and menopause.
TrapOvulation is caused by the LH surge, not FSH or estrogen directly. Endometrium is maintained by progesterone from the corpus luteum.

2.5 Fertilisation and implantation

  • Semen released into the vagina = insemination. Sperms pass through cervix and uterus to the ampullary region of the fallopian tube, where fertilisation occurs — only if ovum and sperm reach it simultaneously.
  • Sperm contacts the zona pellucida → membrane changes block entry of additional sperms (only one sperm fertilises).
  • Acrosome secretions help the sperm enter the ovum cytoplasm → secondary oocyte completes meiosis II (unequal) → second polar body + haploid ovum (ootid) → fusion of nuclei → diploid zygote.
Sex determinationFemale XX, male XY. All ova carry X; 50% sperms carry X and 50% carry Y. XX zygote → female, XY → male. Hence the sex of the baby is determined by the father, not the mother.
Zygote→Cleavage (in isthmus) → blastomeres→Morula (8–16 cells)→Blastocyst→Implantation
  • Blastocyst: outer trophoblast + inner cell mass. Trophoblast attaches to the endometrium; inner cell mass differentiates into the embryo.
  • Uterine cells divide rapidly and cover the blastocyst → it becomes embedded in the endometrium = implantation → pregnancy.

2.6 Pregnancy and embryonic development

  • After implantation, finger-like chorionic villi appear on the trophoblast; interdigitated with uterine tissue they form the placenta, the structural and functional unit between foetus and mother.
  • Placenta supplies oxygen and nutrients and removes CO2 and wastes; connected to the embryo by the umbilical cord.

  • Inner cell mass → ectoderm and endoderm; mesoderm appears between them. These three layers form all adult tissues. The inner cell mass contains stem cells able to give rise to all tissues and organs.

2.7 Parturition and lactation

  • Average human pregnancy ≈ 9 months = gestation period. Delivery of the foetus = parturition, induced by a complex neuroendocrine mechanism.
Signals from fully developed foetus + placenta→Mild contractions: foetal ejection reflex→Oxytocin from maternal pituitary→Stronger uterine contractions → more oxytocin→Baby expelled; then placenta
  • Mammary glands differentiate during pregnancy and start producing milk towards its end = lactation.
  • Milk of the initial few days = colostrum, containing several antibodies essential to develop resistance in new-borns. Breast-feeding is recommended.
TrapParturition signals originate from the foetus and placenta, not the mother. It is a positive feedback loop between uterine contraction and oxytocin.

★ Quick revision

  1. Events: gametogenesis → insemination → fertilisation → implantation → gestation → parturition; humans are viviparous.
  2. Scrotum keeps testes 2–2.5 °C cooler; testis 4–5 × 2–3 cm; ~250 lobules, 1–3 seminiferous tubules each.
  3. Spermatogonia + Sertoli cells (nutrition) line tubules; Leydig cells (androgens) in interstitial spaces.
  4. Ducts: rete testis → vasa efferentia → epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra → urethral meatus.
  5. Glands: paired seminal vesicles, single prostate, paired bulbourethral; seminal plasma rich in fructose, calcium, enzymes.
  6. Ovary 2–4 cm (cortex, medulla); oviduct 10–12 cm: infundibulum (fimbriae), ampulla, isthmus.
  7. Uterus: inverted pear; perimetrium, myometrium (contracts at delivery), endometrium (cyclic changes); cervical canal + vagina = birth canal.
  8. Mammary gland: 15–20 lobes, alveoli → tubules → duct → ampulla → lactiferous duct.
  9. Spermatogenesis: spermatogonium (46) → primary spermatocyte → meiosis I → 2 secondary spermatocytes (23) → 4 spermatids → spermiogenesis → sperms; release = spermiation.
  10. GnRH → LH (Leydig → androgens) and FSH (Sertoli → spermiogenesis factors).
  11. Sperm: head (haploid nucleus + acrosome enzymes), neck, middle piece (mitochondria), tail. 200–300 million per ejaculate; ≥60% normal shape, ≥40% vigorous motility.
  12. Oogenesis starts in foetal life; primary oocytes arrested in prophase I; 60,000–80,000 follicles per ovary at puberty.
  13. Primary follicle → secondary (theca) → tertiary (antrum, theca interna/externa) → Graafian; meiosis I gives secondary oocyte + 1st polar body; zona pellucida; ovulation.
  14. Menstrual cycle ~28/29 days: menstrual (3–5 days) → follicular (estrogen, endometrium proliferates) → ovulation (LH surge ~day 14) → luteal (corpus luteum, progesterone).
  15. Menarche at puberty; menopause ~50 years; no cycles during pregnancy.
  16. Fertilisation in ampulla; zona pellucida blocks polyspermy; acrosome helps entry; meiosis II completes → 2nd polar body + ootid; zygote.
  17. Father determines sex (X or Y sperm).
  18. Cleavage in isthmus → morula (8–16) → blastocyst (trophoblast + inner cell mass) → implantation in endometrium.
  19. Placenta from chorionic villi + uterine tissue; umbilical cord; hCG, hPL, estrogens, progestogens; relaxin from ovary; hCG, hPL, relaxin only in pregnancy.
  20. Germ layers: ectoderm, endoderm, then mesoderm; inner cell mass has stem cells.
  21. Heart at 1 month; limbs and digits by 2 months; organ systems by 12 weeks; movement and head hair in 5th month; fine hair, eyelids separate, eyelashes by 24 weeks.
  22. Parturition: foetal ejection reflex → oxytocin → stronger contractions (positive feedback); colostrum has antibodies.
