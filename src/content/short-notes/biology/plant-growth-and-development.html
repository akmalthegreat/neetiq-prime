13.1 Growth

Growth is regarded as one of the most fundamental and conspicuous characteristics of a living being.

DefinitionGrowth is an irreversible permanent increase in size of an organ or its parts or even of an individual cell. It is generally accompanied by metabolic processes (both anabolic and catabolic) that occur at the expense of energy.
TrapSwelling of a leaf or a seed by absorbing water is an increase in size but is reversible, so it is not growth. The key word is irreversible.

13.1.1 Plant growth is generally indeterminate

  • Plants retain the capacity for unlimited growth throughout their life, because of meristems at certain locations in their body.
  • Meristem cells can divide and self-perpetuate. The product soon loses the capacity to divide, and such cells make up the plant body.
  • This form of growth, where new cells are always being added to the plant body by the activity of the meristem, is called the open form of growth.

Primary growth

  • By root apical meristem and shoot apical meristem
  • Responsible for elongation of the plant along its axis

Secondary growth

  • By lateral meristems: vascular cambium and cork cambium
  • Appear later in life in dicots and gymnosperms
  • Cause increase in girth of organs

13.1.2 Growth is measurable

  • At the cellular level, growth is principally a consequence of increase in the amount of protoplasm. Since this is hard to measure directly, we measure some quantity more or less proportional to it.
  • Parameters: increase in fresh weight, dry weight, length, area, volume and cell number.
Example (NCERT)Growth expressed as
One single maize root apical meristemMore than 17,500 new cells per hour (increase in cell number)
Cells in a watermelonIncrease in size by up to 3,50,000 times (increase in cell size)
Pollen tubeIncrease in length

13.1.3 Phases of growth

The period of growth is divided into three phases: meristematic, elongation and maturation. These are clearly seen in a root tip.

Meristematic→Elongation→Maturation
PhaseLocationFeatures
MeristematicRoot apex and shoot apex (constantly dividing cells)Rich in protoplasm, possess large conspicuous nuclei; cell walls are primary in nature, thin and cellulosic with abundant plasmodesmatal connections
ElongationZone just next (away from the tip) to the meristematic zoneIncreased vacuolation, cell enlargement and new cell wall deposition
MaturationFurther away from apex, proximal to the elongation zoneCells attain their maximal size in terms of wall thickening and protoplasmic modifications
Memory trickRoot tip from the apex upwards: “M-E-M”: Meristematic → Elongation → Maturation. Vacuoles appear in the E zone; thick walls in the last M zone.

13.1.4 Growth rates

The increased growth per unit time is termed the growth rate. It can be arithmetic or geometrical.

Arithmetic growth

  • Following mitotic cell division, only one daughter cell continues to divide while the other differentiates and matures
  • Simplest example: a root elongating at a constant rate
  • Plot of length vs time gives a linear curve
  • Equation: Lt = L0 + rt

Geometric growth

  • Both daughter cells retain the ability to divide and continue to do so
  • Initial growth is slow (lag phase), then rapid, exponential (log / exponential phase)
  • Limited nutrient supply slows growth → stationary phase
  • Plot gives a sigmoid (S-shaped) curve
  • Equation: W1 = W0 ert
SymbolArithmetic: Lt = L0 + rtGeometric: W1 = W0 ert
FinalLt = length at time ‘t’W1 = final size (weight, height, number etc.)
InitialL0 = length at time ‘zero’W0 = initial size at the beginning of the period
rGrowth rate / elongation per unit timeRelative growth rate; also the measure of the ability of the plant to produce new plant material, referred to as efficiency index
Otherst = timet = time of growth; e = base of natural logarithms
Key ideaThe final size of W1 depends on the initial size W0. A sigmoid curve is characteristic of a living organism growing in a natural environment; it is typical for all cells, tissues and organs of a plant.

Absolute vs relative growth rate

Absolute growth rate

  • Measurement and comparison of total growth per unit time

Relative growth rate

  • Growth of the given system per unit time expressed on a common basis, e.g. per unit initial parameter

ConditionWhy it is needed
WaterPlant cells grow in size by cell enlargement, which requires water; turgidity of cells helps extension growth. Water also provides the medium for enzymatic activities needed for growth. Plant growth and further development are intimately linked to the water status of the plant.
OxygenHelps in releasing metabolic energy essential for growth activities
Nutrients (macro and micro essential elements)Required for the synthesis of protoplasm and act as a source of energy
TemperatureEvery plant organism has an optimum temperature range best suited for its growth; any deviation could be detrimental to its survival
Light and gravityEnvironmental signals that also affect certain phases/stages of growth

13.2 Differentiation, dedifferentiation and redifferentiation

ProcessMeaningExample
DifferentiationCells derived from root apical and shoot apical meristems and cambium differentiate and mature to perform specific functionsTo form a tracheary element, cells lose their protoplasm and develop a very strong, elastic, lignocellulosic secondary cell wall to carry water to long distances even under extreme tension
DedifferentiationLiving differentiated cells that by now have lost the capacity to divide can regain the capacity of division under certain conditionsFormation of meristems: interfascicular cambium and cork cambium from fully differentiated parenchyma cells
RedifferentiationCells produced by dedifferentiated tissues once again lose the capacity to divide but mature to perform specific functionsSecondary xylem, secondary phloem, phellem (cork) etc. formed by the dedifferentiated cambia
Key ideaPlant growth is open, and so is differentiation: cells/tissues arising out of the same meristem have different structures at maturity. The final structure at maturity of a cell/tissue is also determined by the location of the cell within. E.g. cells positioned away from the root apical meristem differentiate as root-cap cells, while those pushed to the periphery mature as epidermis.
TrapInterfascicular cambium and cork cambium are secondary meristems formed by dedifferentiation, not differentiation. The tissues they produce are the result of redifferentiation.
Memory trickD → De → Re: Differentiation = lose the power to divide; Dedifferentiation = regain it; Redifferentiation = lose it again.

13.3 Development

DefinitionDevelopment is a term that includes all changes that an organism goes through during its life cycle from germination of the seed to senescence.

Sequence of developmental processes in a plant cell:

Meristematic cell→Cell division→Plasmatic growth→Expansion / elongation→Differentiation→Mature cell

A mature cell can dedifferentiate (regain division) and its products can redifferentiate, making the sequence cyclic in some tissues. The whole life cycle runs from seed germination → vegetative growth → reproductive phase → senescence → death.

Plasticity

DefinitionPlants follow different pathways in response to environment or phases of life to form different kinds of structures. This ability is called plasticity.

Example of plasticity: heterophylly (different leaf shapes on the same plant).

Type of heterophyllyExamples (NCERT)
Due to phase of life (leaves of the juvenile plant differ in shape from those in mature plants)Cotton, coriander and larkspur
Due to environment (leaves produced in air differ from those produced in water)Buttercup
TrapNEET frequently swaps these: buttercup = environmental heterophylly; cotton, coriander, larkspur = developmental (life-phase) heterophylly.
Key ideaDevelopment = growth + differentiation. Both are under the control of intrinsic and extrinsic factors.

Intrinsic factors

  • Intracellular: genetic
  • Intercellular: chemicals such as plant growth regulators

Extrinsic factors

  • Light, temperature, water, oxygen, nutrition etc.

13.4 Plant growth regulators

13.4.1 Characteristics

  • PGRs are small, simple molecules of diverse chemical composition.
  • Also called plant growth substances, plant hormones or phytohormones.
Chemical naturePGR
Indole compoundsIndole-3-acetic acid (IAA) — auxin
Adenine derivativesN6-furfurylamino purine (kinetin) — cytokinin
Derivatives of carotenoidsAbscisic acid (ABA)
TerpenesGibberellic acid (GA3)
GasesEthylene (C2H4)
Memory trick“I-A-C-T-G” → Indole = IAA (auxin); Adenine = kinetin (cytokinin); Carotenoid = ABA; Terpene = GA; Gas = ethylene.

Two broad groups by function

Plant growth promoters

  • Involved in growth-promoting activities: cell division, cell enlargement, pattern formation, tropic growth, flowering, fruiting and seed formation
  • Auxins, gibberellins and cytokinins

Plant growth inhibitors

  • Responses to wounds and stresses of biotic and abiotic origin; inhibitory activities like dormancy and abscission
  • Abscisic acid (ABA)
NEET HighlightThe gaseous PGR ethylene could fit either group, but it is largely an inhibitor of growth activities.

13.4.2 The discovery of plant growth regulators

Interestingly, the discovery of each of the five major groups of PGRs was accidental.

PGRScientist(s)Observation
AuxinCharles Darwin and his son Francis DarwinObserved that the coleoptiles of canary grass responded to unilateral illumination by growing towards the light source (phototropism). After a series of experiments it was concluded that the tip of the coleoptile was the site of transmittable influence that caused bending of the entire coleoptile.
Auxin (isolated)F.W. WentIsolated auxin from tips of coleoptiles of oat seedlings
GibberellinE. Kurosawa‘Bakanae’ (foolish seedling) disease of rice seedlings caused by the fungal pathogen Gibberella fujikuroi. Kurosawa reported the appearance of symptoms of the disease in uninfected rice seedlings when they were treated with sterile filtrates of the fungus. The active substance was later identified as gibberellic acid.
Cytokinin (kinetin)F. Skoog and his co-workersFrom the internodal segments of tobacco stems, the callus (a mass of undifferentiated cells) proliferated only if, in addition to auxins, the nutrient medium was supplemented with one of the following: extracts of vascular tissues, yeast extract, coconut milk or DNA. Skoog and Miller later identified and crystallised the cytokinesis-promoting active substance that they termed kinetin.
Abscisic acidThree independent researches (mid-1960s)Reported the purification and chemical characterisation of three different kinds of inhibitors: inhibitor-B, abscission II and dormin. Later all three were proved to be chemically identical → named abscisic acid (ABA).
EthyleneCousins (1910)Confirmed the release of a volatile substance from ripened oranges that hastened the ripening of stored unripened bananas. This volatile substance was later identified as ethylene, a gaseous PGR.
TrapCanary grass → Darwins (phototropism); oat coleoptile → Went (auxin isolation). Oranges ripen bananas (not the other way round). Tobacco callus → kinetin. Don’t mix the plants!

13.4.3.1 Auxins

  • Auxins (Greek ‘auxein’: to grow) were first isolated from human urine.
  • The term ‘auxin’ is applied to indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) and to other natural and synthetic compounds having certain growth regulating properties.
  • Generally produced by the growing apices of the stems and roots, from where they migrate to the regions of their action.

Natural auxins

  • IAA (indole-3-acetic acid)
  • IBA (indole butyric acid)
  • Isolated from plants

Synthetic auxins

  • NAA (naphthalene acetic acid)
  • 2,4-D (2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid)

Functions and applications of auxins

RoleDetails
RootingHelp to initiate rooting in stem cuttings, an application widely used for plant propagation
FloweringPromote flowering, e.g. in pineapples
AbscissionHelp to prevent fruit and leaf drop at early stages but promote the abscission of older mature leaves and fruits
Apical dominanceIn most higher plants, the growing apical bud inhibits the growth of the lateral (axillary) buds, a phenomenon called apical dominance. Removal of shoot tips (decapitation) usually results in the growth of lateral buds. Widely applied in tea plantations and hedge-making.
ParthenocarpyInduce parthenocarpy, e.g. in tomatoes
Herbicides2,4-D is widely used to kill dicotyledonous weeds; does not affect mature monocotyledonous plants. Used by gardeners to prepare weed-free lawns.
OthersControl xylem differentiation and help in cell division
NEET HighlightAuxin plays a dual role in abscission: it prevents early drop of young fruits/leaves but promotes abscission of older leaves and fruits. Questions often test only one half.
Trap2,4-D kills dicot weeds; lawns (grass) are monocots, so they survive. 2,4-D is synthetic; IBA is natural (a common distractor lists IBA as synthetic).

13.4.3.2 Gibberellins

  • Another kind of promotory PGR. There are more than 100 gibberellins reported from widely different organisms such as fungi and higher plants.
  • Denoted as GA1, GA2, GA3 and so on.
  • Gibberellic acid (GA3) was one of the first gibberellins to be discovered and remains the most intensively studied form.
  • All GAs are acidic.

Functions and applications

EffectDetails
Increase in axis lengthAbility to cause an increase in length of axis is used to increase the length of grapes stalks
Fruit shapeCause fruits like apple to elongate and improve in shape
Delay senescenceFruits can be left on the tree longer so as to extend the market period
BrewingGA3 is used to speed up the malting process in the brewing industry
SugarcaneSugarcane stores carbohydrate as sugar in its stems. Spraying sugarcane crop with gibberellins increases the length of the stem, thus increasing the yield by as much as 20 tonnes per acre.
ConifersSpraying juvenile conifers with GAs hastens maturity, leading to early seed production
BoltingPromote bolting (internode elongation just prior to flowering) in beet, cabbages and many plants with rosette habit
Memory trickGA = “Grow Axis”: grapes stalks, sugarcane stems, bolting in cabbage — all are length increases.
TrapBolting is by gibberellins, not auxins or cytokinins. Malting in brewing uses GA3. Sugarcane yield increase is 20 tonnes per acre (not per hectare).

13.4.3.3 Cytokinins

  • Cytokinins have specific effects on cytokinesis (cell division).
  • Discovered as kinetin (a modified form of adenine, a purine) from the autoclaved herring sperm DNA.
  • Kinetin does not occur naturally in plants.
  • Search for natural substances with cytokinin-like activities led to the isolation of zeatin from corn-kernels and coconut milk.
  • Since the discovery of zeatin, several naturally occurring cytokinins and some synthetic compounds with cell division promoting activity have been identified.
  • Natural cytokinins are synthesised in regions where rapid cell division occurs, e.g. root apices, developing shoot buds, young fruits etc.

Functions

  • Help to produce new leaves, chloroplasts in leaves, lateral shoot growth and adventitious shoot formation.
  • Help overcome apical dominance.
  • Promote nutrient mobilisation, which helps in the delay of leaf senescence.
TrapKinetin = synthetic/animal-DNA-derived and not natural in plants; zeatin = natural cytokinin (corn kernels, coconut milk). Auxin causes apical dominance; cytokinin overcomes it.
Key ideaAuxin : cytokinin balance controls tissue-culture morphogenesis — auxin favours roots, cytokinin favours shoots (this is why Skoog’s callus needed both).

13.4.3.4 Ethylene

  • A simple gaseous PGR. Synthesised in large amounts by tissues undergoing senescence and by ripening fruits.

Influences of ethylene on plants

AreaEffect
SeedlingsHorizontal growth of seedlings, swelling of the axis and apical hook formation in dicot seedlings
Senescence and abscissionPromotes senescence and abscission of plant organs, especially of leaves and flowers
Fruit ripeningHighly effective in fruit ripening. Enhances the respiration rate during ripening of the fruits; this rise in rate of respiration is called respiratory climactic.
Dormancy and germinationBreaks seed and bud dormancy; initiates germination in peanut seeds, sprouting of potato tubers
Deep water ricePromotes rapid internode/petiole elongation in deep water rice plants; it helps leaves/upper parts of the shoot to remain above water
RootsPromotes root growth and root hair formation, thus helping plants to increase their absorption surface
FloweringUsed to initiate flowering and for synchronising fruit-set in pineapples. Also induces flowering in mango.

Ethephon

  • Since ethylene regulates so many physiological processes, it is one of the most widely used PGRs in agriculture.
  • The most widely used compound as a source of ethylene is ethephon. In an aqueous solution it is readily absorbed and transported within the plant and releases ethylene slowly.
  • Ethephon hastens fruit ripening in tomatoes and apples and accelerates abscission in flowers and fruits (thinning of cotton, cherry, walnut).
  • Promotes female flowers in cucumbers, thereby increasing the yield.
NEET HighlightPineapple appears with two hormones: auxin promotes flowering in pineapple, and ethylene initiates flowering and synchronises fruit-set in pineapple. Read the option carefully.
TrapRespiratory climactic (rise in respiration during ripening) is linked to ethylene. Ethylene breaks dormancy (peanut seeds, potato tubers) — the opposite of ABA.

13.4.3.5 Abscisic acid

ABA was discovered for its role in regulating abscission and dormancy. Like other PGRs it has other wide-ranging effects on plant growth and development.

  • Acts as a general plant growth inhibitor and an inhibitor of plant metabolism.
  • Inhibits seed germination.
  • Stimulates the closure of stomata.
  • Increases the tolerance of plants to various kinds of stresses; therefore it is also called the stress hormone.
  • Plays an important role in seed development, maturation and dormancy. By inducing dormancy, ABA helps seeds to withstand desiccation and other factors unfavourable for growth.
  • In most situations, ABA acts as an antagonist to GAs.
Memory trickABA = “Always Brakes Activity”: stops germination, shuts stomata, keeps seeds dormant → the stress hormone.

13.4.4 PGRs act together

  • For any and every phase of growth, differentiation and development of plants, one or the other PGR has some role to play. Such roles could be complementary or antagonistic; they could be individualistic or synergistic.
  • There are a number of events in plant life where more than one PGR interacts to affect that event, e.g. dormancy in seeds/buds, abscission, senescence, apical dominance etc.
  • PGR role is only one kind of intrinsic control. Along with genomic control and extrinsic factors, they play an important role in plant growth and development.

★ Quick revision

  1. Growth = irreversible permanent increase in size; accompanied by metabolism at the expense of energy.
  2. Plants show open (indeterminate) growth due to meristems; primary growth = elongation (apical meristems); secondary growth = girth (vascular + cork cambium) in dicots and gymnosperms.
  3. Growth measured as fresh weight, dry weight, length, area, volume, cell number.
  4. Maize root apical meristem: >17,500 new cells/hour; watermelon cells enlarge up to 3,50,000 times; pollen tube → length; dorsiventral leaf → surface area.
  5. Phases: meristematic (dense protoplasm, large nuclei, thin cellulosic primary walls, many plasmodesmata) → elongation (vacuolation, enlargement, new wall deposition) → maturation (maximal size, wall thickening).
  6. Arithmetic growth: one daughter cell divides; linear; Lt = L0 + rt (constant root elongation).
  7. Geometric growth: both daughter cells divide; lag → log → stationary; sigmoid; W1 = W0ert; r = relative growth rate = efficiency index.
  8. Absolute growth = total growth per unit time; relative = per unit initial parameter (leaf A 5→10 cm² = 100%; leaf B 50→55 cm² = 10%).
  9. Conditions: water (turgidity, enlargement), oxygen (energy), nutrients, optimum temperature, light and gravity.
  10. Differentiation: tracheary elements lose protoplasm and form lignocellulosic secondary walls.
  11. Dedifferentiation: parenchyma → interfascicular cambium, cork cambium; redifferentiation: their products lose division ability again.
  12. Differentiation is open; final structure depends on location (root-cap vs epidermis).
  13. Development = growth + differentiation; all changes from germination to senescence.
  14. Plasticity → heterophylly: life-phase (cotton, coriander, larkspur); environment (buttercup — air vs water).
  15. Intrinsic control: genetic + PGRs; extrinsic: light, temperature, water, oxygen, nutrition.
  16. Chemical nature: IAA = indole; kinetin = adenine derivative; ABA = carotenoid derivative; GA3 = terpene; ethylene = gas.
  17. Promoters: auxin, GA, cytokinin; inhibitor: ABA; ethylene: largely an inhibitor.
  18. Darwins: canary grass coleoptile phototropism; Went: auxin from oat coleoptile tips; Kurosawa: bakanae of rice by Gibberella fujikuroi; Skoog: tobacco callus, Skoog & Miller: kinetin; inhibitor-B, abscission II, dormin = ABA; Cousins: oranges ripen bananas (ethylene).
  19. Auxin first isolated from human urine; natural IAA, IBA; synthetic NAA, 2,4-D.
  20. Auxin: rooting of stem cuttings, flowering in pineapple, prevents early fruit/leaf drop but promotes abscission of old organs, apical dominance (tea, hedges), parthenocarpy (tomato), 2,4-D weedicide (dicot weeds), xylem differentiation.
  21. GA: >100 types, all acidic, GA3 most studied; grapes stalks, apple shape, delays senescence, malting (brewing), sugarcane +20 tonnes/acre, early seeds in juvenile conifers, bolting in beet and cabbage.
  22. Cytokinin: kinetin from autoclaved herring sperm DNA (not natural in plants); zeatin from corn kernels and coconut milk; made in root apices, shoot buds, young fruits.
  23. Cytokinin: new leaves, chloroplasts, lateral and adventitious shoots, overcomes apical dominance, delays leaf senescence via nutrient mobilisation.
  24. Ethylene: horizontal seedling growth, axis swelling, apical hook; senescence and abscission; fruit ripening with respiratory climactic; breaks dormancy (peanut seed, potato tuber); deep-water rice elongation; root hairs; flowering in pineapple and mango.
  25. Ethephon releases ethylene slowly: ripens tomato and apple, thins cotton, cherry and walnut, more female flowers in cucumber.
  26. ABA: growth and metabolism inhibitor, inhibits germination, closes stomata, stress hormone, seed maturation and dormancy (withstand desiccation); antagonist of GA.
  27. PGR interactions can be complementary, antagonistic, individualistic or synergistic.
