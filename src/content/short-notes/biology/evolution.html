6.1 Origin of life

  • Big Bang theory explains the origin of the universe, about 20 billion years old. Earth formed about 4.5 billion years back.
  • Early earth had no atmosphere; water vapour, methane, CO2 and ammonia released from the molten mass covered the surface. UV rays split water into H2 and O2; lighter H2 escaped; O2 combined with NH3 and CH4 to form water, CO2 etc.; the ozone layer formed; as it cooled, water vapour fell as rain.
  • Life appeared 500 million years after earth formed, i.e., almost four billion years back.
TheoryIdea
PanspermiaUnits of life (spores) were transferred to earth from other planets; still a favourite idea of some astronomers
Spontaneous generationLife came from decaying and rotting matter (straw, mud). Disproved by Louis Pasteur: in pre-sterilised flasks life did not arise from killed yeast, but in a flask open to air new organisms arose → life comes only from pre-existing life
Chemical evolution (Oparin–Haldane)Oparin (Russia) and Haldane (England): first life came from pre-existing non-living organic molecules (RNA, protein etc.); preceded by chemical evolution — organic molecules from inorganic ones. Conditions: high temperature, volcanic storms, reducing atmosphere with CH4, NH3 etc.
S.L. Miller’s experiment (1953)Electric discharge in a closed flask containing CH4, H2, NH3 and water vapour at 800 °C → formation of amino acids. Others observed sugars, nitrogen bases, pigments and fats. Analysis of meteorites showed similar compounds.
  • First non-cellular forms of life could have originated 3 billion years back (giant molecules: RNA, protein, polysaccharides). The first cellular forms did not originate till about 2000 million years ago, probably single cells, in water.
TrapMiller’s mixture had no free oxygen (reducing). Temperature 800 °C. He got amino acids, not proteins or cells.

6.2 Evolution of life forms — a theory

Special creation (3 connotations)(i) All living organisms were created as such; (ii) diversity was always the same and will remain so; (iii) earth is about 4000 years old. Challenged in the 19th century.
  • Charles Darwin, from observations during a sea voyage on HMS Beagle, concluded that living forms share similarities among themselves and with forms that existed millions of years ago; many no longer exist.
  • Any population has built-in variation; individuals better fit to survive leave more progeny. Fitness, according to Darwin, refers ultimately and only to reproductive fitness. They are selected by nature → natural selection.
  • Alfred Wallace, working in the Malay Archipelago (present Indonesia), reached similar conclusions around the same time.

6.3 What are the evidences for evolution?

Paleontological evidence

  • Fossils are remains of hard parts of life-forms found in rocks. Different-aged sedimentary layers contain different life-forms; some represent extinct organisms (e.g. dinosaurs). Age of fossils is found by radioactive dating.

Comparative anatomy and morphology

Homologous organsAnalogous organs

Animal examplesForelimbs of whale, bat, cheetah, human (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges); vertebrate hearts and brainsWings of butterfly and bird; eye of octopus and mammals; flippers of penguins and dolphins
Plant examplesThorn and tendrils of Bougainvillea and CucurbitaSweet potato (root modification) and potato (stem modification)
TrapThorn of Bougainvillea and tendril of Cucurbita = homologous. Sweet potato vs potato = analogous. Octopus eye vs mammal eye = analogous.

Biochemical evidence

Similarities in proteins and genes performing a given function among diverse organisms give clues to common ancestry.

Natural selection in action: industrial melanism

EnglandMoths observedReason
1850s (before industrialisation)More white-winged mothsTrees covered by white lichens; dark moths picked out by predators
1920 (after industrialisation)More dark-winged (melanised) mothsTree trunks darkened by smoke and soot; white moths eaten
  • Moths able to camouflage survived. In rural areas the count of melanic moths stayed low. No variant is completely wiped out.
  • Lichens are industrial pollution indicators — they do not grow in polluted areas.
  • Resistance to herbicides, pesticides, antibiotics and drugs appears in months or years = evolution by anthropogenic action.

6.4 What is adaptive radiation?

DefinitionEvolution of different species in a given geographical area starting from a point and literally radiating to other areas (habitats) = adaptive radiation.
ExampleDetail
Darwin’s finchesGalapagos Islands; from original seed-eating finches, forms with altered beaks arose → insectivorous and vegetarian finches
Australian marsupialsMany different marsupials evolved from an ancestral stock within the Australian island continent
Convergent evolutionWhen more than one adaptive radiation occurs in an isolated area, it can be called convergent evolution: Australian placental mammals resemble corresponding marsupials, e.g. placental wolf and Tasmanian wolf (marsupial).

TrapDarwinian variations = small, directional; de Vries’ mutations = large, random, directionless. Saltation is de Vries’ term.

6.7 Hardy-Weinberg principle

  • Allele frequencies in a population are stable and constant from generation to generation; the gene pool remains constant = genetic equilibrium. Sum of all allelic frequencies = 1.
Equationp² + 2pq + q² = 1 — binomial expansion of (p + q)². p = frequency of A, q = frequency of a; AA = p², Aa = 2pq, aa = q².

A measured frequency different from the expected value indicates the extent of evolutionary change.

Factor disturbing equilibriumMeaning
Gene migration / gene flowSection of population migrates → frequencies change in both; repeated migration = gene flow
Genetic driftSame change occurring by chance; if the new sample becomes a different species, the drifted population are founders → founder effect
MutationPre-existing advantageous mutations, when selected, give new phenotypes and over generations speciation
Genetic recombinationVariation during gametogenesis
Natural selectionHeritable variations enabling better survival leave more progeny
Memory trickFive factors: “My Grandma Gets Rich Naturally” → Mutation, Gene flow, Genetic drift, Recombination, Natural selection.
  • Stabilisation: more individuals acquire the mean character value. Directional: more acquire a value other than the mean. Disruption: more acquire peripheral values at both ends.

6.8 A brief account of evolution

TimeEvent (NCERT)
~2000 myaFirst cellular life forms; some released O2 (like the light reaction)
~500 myaInvertebrates formed and active
~350 myaJawless fish; fish with stout, strong fins could move on land (lobefins)
~320 myaSea weeds and a few plants; plants were the first to invade land
~200 myaSome land reptiles went back to water → fish-like reptiles (Ichthyosaurs)
~65 myaDinosaurs suddenly disappeared (climate change? evolved into birds?)
  • Coelacanth, caught in South Africa in 1938, was thought to be extinct. Lobefins evolved into the first amphibians, ancestors of frogs and salamanders.
  • Amphibians → reptiles, which lay thick-shelled eggs that do not dry up; modern descendants: turtles, tortoises, crocodiles. Reptiles dominated for about 200 million years; giant ferns (pteridophytes) fell to form coal deposits.
  • Tyrannosaurus rex: biggest dinosaur, about 20 feet tall, dagger-like teeth.
  • First mammals were shrew-like; mammals are viviparous and more intelligent. Due to continental drift, South American mammals (horse-, hippo-, bear-, rabbit-like) were overridden by North American fauna, while Australian pouched mammals survived due to lack of competition.
TrapThe first organisms to invade land were plants, not animals. Coelacanth = lobefin, rediscovered in 1938.
