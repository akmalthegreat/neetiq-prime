8.2 Microbes in industrial products

8.2.1 Fermented beverages

  • Saccharomyces cerevisiae (brewer’s yeast) ferments malted cereals and fruit juices to produce ethanol.

Without distillation

  • Wine, beer

With distillation of fermented broth

  • Whisky, brandy, rum

8.2.2 Antibiotics

  • Antibiotics (anti = against, bio = life) are chemicals produced by some microbes that kill or retard the growth of other (disease-causing) microbes — one of the most significant discoveries of the 20th century.
  • Penicillin was the first antibiotic, a chance discovery by Alexander Fleming: while working on Staphylococci, he saw a mould in an unwashed plate around which they could not grow. Named after Penicillium notatum.
  • Full potential established by Ernest Chain and Howard Florey; used for soldiers in World War II. Fleming, Chain and Florey: Nobel Prize 1945.
  • Antibiotics help treat plague, whooping cough (kali khansi), diphtheria (gal ghotu) and leprosy (kusht rog).

8.3 Microbes in sewage treatment

Municipal waste-water = sewage; major component is human excreta; rich in organic matter and pathogenic microbes; treated in sewage treatment plants (STPs) before release.

StageWhat happens
Primary (physical)Physical removal of particles by filtration (floating debris) and sedimentation (grit — soil, small pebbles). Settled solids = primary sludge; supernatant = effluent.
Secondary (biological)Effluent in large aeration tanks, agitated and aerated → aerobic microbes grow as flocs (bacteria + fungal filaments, mesh-like), consuming organic matter → BOD reduced.
Settling tankFlocs sediment = activated sludge; a small part goes back to the aeration tank as inoculum; the rest goes to anaerobic sludge digesters.
Anaerobic sludge digesterAnaerobic bacteria digest the bacteria and fungi in sludge, producing methane, hydrogen sulphide and CO2 = biogas (inflammable)
BODBiochemical oxygen demand = amount of oxygen consumed if all organic matter in one litre of water were oxidised by bacteria. Greater BOD → more polluting potential. It is an indirect measure of organic matter.

The Ministry of Environment and Forests started the Ganga Action Plan and Yamuna Action Plan to build STPs so that only treated sewage enters rivers.

TrapPrimary treatment is physical; secondary is biological (aerobic). Biogas is made anaerobically in the sludge digester. High BOD = dirty water.

8.4 Microbes in production of biogas

  • Biogas: mixture of gases, predominantly methane, produced by microbes; used as fuel.
  • Methanogens (e.g. Methanobacterium) grow anaerobically on cellulosic material, producing CH4 with CO2 and H2. Found in anaerobic sludge and in the rumen of cattle, where they help digest cellulose. Cattle dung (gobar) is rich in them → gobar gas.
  • Plant: concrete tank 10–15 feet deep; slurry of dung fed in; a floating cover rises as gas is produced; gas pipe supplies houses; spent slurry used as fertiliser. Used for cooking and lighting in rural areas.
  • Technology developed in India mainly by IARI (Indian Agricultural Research Institute) and KVIC (Khadi and Village Industries Commission).

8.5 Microbes as biocontrol agents

Biocontrol = biological methods for controlling plant diseases and pests, reducing dependence on toxic chemicals. An organic farmer holds that biodiversity furthers health; complete eradication of pests is undesirable because beneficial predators and parasites depend on them.

AgentControls / how
Ladybird (red and black beetle)Aphids
DragonfliesMosquitoes
Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)Butterfly caterpillars. Dried spores sold in sachets, mixed with water and sprayed on brassicas and fruit trees; eaten by larvae; toxin released in the gut kills them, leaving other insects unharmed. Bt toxin genes put into plants → Bt-cotton.
Trichoderma (fungus)Free-living in root ecosystems; biocontrol of several plant pathogens (plant disease)
Baculoviruses (Nucleopolyhedrovirus)Attack insects and arthropods; species-specific, narrow spectrum; no harm to plants, mammals, birds, fish or non-target insects; ideal for IPM (integrated pest management) and ecologically sensitive areas
TrapLadybird → aphids; dragonfly → mosquitoes. Trichoderma controls plant pathogens (diseases), not insects. Baculoviruses are narrow-spectrum.

8.6 Microbes as biofertilisers

Biofertilisers are organisms that enrich the nutrient quality of soil: bacteria, fungi and cyanobacteria.

BiofertiliserExamplesRole
Symbiotic bacteriaRhizobium in root nodules of legumesFix atmospheric nitrogen into organic forms
Free-living bacteriaAzospirillum, AzotobacterFix nitrogen while free-living in soil
Fungi — mycorrhizaMany members of GlomusAbsorb phosphorus from soil and pass it to the plant; also give resistance to root-borne pathogens, tolerance to salinity and drought, better growth
