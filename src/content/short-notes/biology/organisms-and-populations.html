11.1 Populations — attributes

DefinitionA population is a group of individuals of the same species living in a well-defined geographical area, sharing or competing for similar resources, and potentially interbreeding.

A population has attributes that an individual does not: birth rate, death rate, sex ratio and age distribution.

AttributeNCERT example
Birth rate (per capita)Pond had 20 lotus plants; 8 new added → birth rate = 8/20 = 0.4 offspring per lotus per year
Death rate (per capita)4 of 40 fruitflies died in a week → death rate = 4/40 = 0.1 individuals per fruitfly per week
Sex ratioe.g. 60% females, 40% males
Age distributionPlotted as an age pyramid; for humans, males and females in one diagram
Population density (N)Population size is technically called population density (N). Number is not always the best measure: with 200 Parthenium plants and one huge banyan tree, per cent cover or biomass is more meaningful. Relative density may be enough, e.g. number of fish caught per trap. Tiger census uses pug marks and fecal pellets (indirect estimation).

11.1.2 Population growth

Increase densityDecrease density
Natality (B): births added in a periodMortality (D): deaths in a period
Immigration (I): individuals of the same species coming inEmigration (E): individuals leaving
EquationNt+1 = Nt + [(B + I) – (D + E)]. Density increases if (B + I) > (D + E).

Normally births and deaths matter most; immigration matters more when a new habitat is being colonised.

11.1.3 Growth models

Exponential growth

  • When resources (food, space) are unlimited
  • dN/dt = (b – d) × N = rN
  • r = intrinsic rate of natural increase
  • Integral form: Nt = N0 ert (e = 2.71828)
  • J-shaped curve
  • Darwin showed even a slow-growing elephant could reach enormous numbers without checks

Logistic growth

  • Resources limited → competition; maximum number = carrying capacity (K)
  • Lag → acceleration → deceleration → asymptote at K
  • Verhulst-Pearl Logistic Growth: dN/dt = rN[(K – N)/K]
  • Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve
  • Considered more realistic
Organismr value
Norway rat0.015
Flour beetle0.12
Human population in India (1981)0.0205
TrapExponential = J, unlimited resources; logistic = S, limited resources with K. r = b – d (per capita rates). When N = K, dN/dt = 0.

Key ideaPredation, parasitism and commensalism share a feature: the interacting species live closely together.

11.1.5 Predation

  • Nature’s way of transferring energy fixed by plants to higher trophic levels. A sparrow eating a seed is also a predator; herbivores are ecologically similar to predators.
  • Predators keep prey populations under control; exotic species become invasive without natural predators.
  • Prickly pear cactus introduced into Australia (early 1920s) spread over millions of hectares; controlled only after a cactus-feeding moth was introduced.
  • Predators maintain species diversity by reducing competition among prey: removing starfish Pisaster from rocky intertidal communities of the American Pacific Coast led to extinction of more than 10 invertebrate species within a year.
  • Over-efficient predators would drive prey and then themselves extinct → predators are ‘prudent’.
Prey defenceExamples (NCERT)
Camouflage (cryptic colouring)Some insects and frogs
DistastefulnessMonarch butterfly — distasteful to birds due to a chemical acquired in its caterpillar stage by feeding on a poisonous weed
Morphological (plants)Thorns — Acacia, cactus
Chemical (plants)Calotropis makes highly poisonous cardiac glycosides (cattle and goats avoid it); nicotine, caffeine, quinine, strychnine, opium are defences against grazers

Nearly 25% of all insects are phytophagous (feed on plant sap and parts).

11.1.5 Competition

  • Unrelated species can compete: in some shallow South American lakes, visiting flamingoes and resident fishes compete for zooplankton.
  • Resources need not be limiting: in interference competition, one species’ feeding efficiency drops due to the inhibitory presence of another even when resources are abundant.
  • Best defined as a process in which the fitness (r) of one species is significantly lower in the presence of another.
Evidence / conceptDetail (NCERT)
Abingdon tortoiseGalapagos; extinct within a decade after goats were introduced (greater browsing efficiency)
Competitive releaseA restricted species expands its range when the superior competitor is removed. Connell: on rocky coasts of Scotland, larger barnacle Balanus excludes smaller Chathamalus from the intertidal zone.
Gause’s Competitive Exclusion PrincipleTwo closely related species competing for the same resources cannot co-exist indefinitely; the inferior one is eliminated — true if resources are limiting
Resource partitioningAvoiding competition by different feeding times or foraging patterns. MacArthur: five closely related warblers on the same tree co-exist by behavioural differences in foraging.
NEET HighlightHerbivores and plants are more adversely affected by competition than carnivores.
TrapBalanus (larger) wins over Chathamalus (smaller). MacArthur = warblers (resource partitioning). Flamingoes and fish are unrelated competitors.

11.1.5 Parasitism

  • Free lodging and meals. Many parasites are host-specific and co-evolve with their hosts.
  • Adaptations: loss of unnecessary sense organs, adhesive organs or suckers, loss of digestive system, high reproductive capacity.
  • Complex life cycles: human liver fluke (trematode) needs two intermediate hosts — snail and fish; the malarial parasite needs a vector (mosquito).
  • Parasites may reduce host survival, growth, reproduction and population density, and make hosts vulnerable to predation.
TypeExamples (NCERT)
Ectoparasites (external surface)Lice on humans, ticks on dogs, copepods on marine fish; Cuscuta on hedge plants (lost chlorophyll and leaves)
Endoparasites (inside host)Live in liver, kidney, lungs, RBCs; more complex life cycles; simplified morphology, high reproductive potential
Brood parasitismParasitic bird lays eggs in the host’s nest; eggs evolved to resemble the host’s in size and colour. Cuckoo (koel) and crow, spring to summer
TrapThe female mosquito is not considered a parasite, though it needs our blood. Cuscuta is an ectoparasite without chlorophyll.

ExampleDetail (NCERT)
LichensFungus + photosynthesising algae or cyanobacteria
MycorrhizaeFungi help roots absorb nutrients; plant gives carbohydrates
