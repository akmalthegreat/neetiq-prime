3.1 Reproductive health: problems and strategies

Definition (WHO)Reproductive health means a total well-being in all aspects of reproduction, i.e., physical, emotional, behavioural and social.
  • India was amongst the first countries to initiate national action plans for reproductive health. ‘Family planning’ programmes began in 1951.
  • Improved programmes now run as ‘Reproductive and Child Health Care (RCH) programmes’.
  • Awareness through audio-visual and print media, governmental and non-governmental agencies, parents, relatives, teachers and friends; sex education in schools should be encouraged to counter myths and misconceptions.
  • People should know about reproductive organs, adolescence, safe and hygienic sexual practices, STIs (e.g. AIDS), birth control options, care of pregnant mothers, post-natal care, breast feeding, and equal opportunities for male and female children.

3.2 Natural and barrier methods

Natural methods avoid the meeting of ovum and sperm. No medicines or devices → side effects almost nil, but chances of failure are quite high.

Natural methodPrinciple
Periodic abstinenceAvoid coitus from day 10 to 17 of the menstrual cycle, when ovulation is expected — the fertile period
Withdrawal / coitus interruptusMale withdraws the penis just before ejaculation to avoid insemination
Lactational amenorrheaNo ovulation and no cycle during intense lactation after parturition; effective only up to a maximum of six months after parturition, while the mother breast-feeds fully

Barrier methods

  • Condoms: thin rubber/latex sheaths covering the penis (male) or vagina and cervix (female). ‘Nirodh’ is a popular male condom brand. Additional benefit: protection from STIs and AIDS. Disposable, self-inserted, give privacy.
  • Diaphragms, cervical caps and vaults: rubber barriers inserted to cover the cervix; block sperm entry; reusable.
  • Spermicidal creams, jellies and foams are used along with these barriers to increase efficiency.
TrapOnly condoms protect against STIs/AIDS. Condoms are disposable; diaphragms, caps and vaults are reusable. Lactational amenorrhea works only up to 6 months.

3.2 IUDs, oral pills, injectables and implants

IUD typeExamplesAction
Non-medicatedLippes loopIncrease phagocytosis of sperms in the uterus; Cu ions suppress sperm motility and fertilising capacity
Copper releasingCuT, Cu7, Multiload 375
Hormone releasingProgestasert, LNG-20Additionally make the uterus unsuitable for implantation and the cervix hostile to sperms
  • IUDs are inserted by doctors or expert nurses through the vagina; ideal for females who want to delay pregnancy and/or space children; one of the most widely accepted methods in India.

Oral pills

  • Progestogens or progestogen–estrogen combinations taken daily for 21 days, starting preferably within the first five days of the cycle; after a 7-day gap (menstruation) repeated.
  • Action: inhibit ovulation and implantation and alter the quality of cervical mucus to prevent/retard sperm entry. Very effective, fewer side effects.
  • Saheli: non-steroidal, ‘once a week’ pill with very few side effects and high contraceptive value.

Injectables, implants and emergency contraception

  • Progestogens alone or with estrogen as injections or implants under the skin; action like pills but effective for much longer periods.
  • Emergency contraception: progestogens / progestogen–estrogen combinations or IUDs within 72 hours of coitus — to avoid pregnancy after rape or casual unprotected intercourse.
Memory trickPill schedule “21 on, 7 off, start by day 5”. Emergency window: 72 hours.

3.2 Surgical methods (sterilisation)

Vasectomy (male)

  • Small part of the vas deferens removed or tied up through a small incision on the scrotum

Tubectomy (female)

  • Small part of the fallopian tube removed or tied up through a small incision in the abdomen or through the vagina
  • Advised as a terminal method; blocks gamete transport. Very effective but reversibility is very poor.
  • Possible ill-effects of contraceptives: nausea, abdominal pain, breakthrough bleeding, irregular menstrual bleeding or even breast cancer, though not very significant.

3.3 Medical termination of pregnancy (MTP)

  • Intentional or voluntary termination of pregnancy before full term = MTP or induced abortion.
  • Nearly 45 to 50 million MTPs a year worldwide — about 1/5th of all conceived pregnancies.
  • Government of India legalised MTP in 1971 with strict conditions; MTP (Amendment) Act, 2017 aims to reduce illegal abortions and maternal mortality and morbidity.
Duration of pregnancyOpinion required
Within the first 12 weeksOne registered medical practitioner

Grounds: risk to the life of the woman or grave injury to her physical or mental health; or substantial risk that the child would be seriously handicapped by physical or mental abnormalities.

NEET HighlightMTPs are relatively safe in the first trimester (up to 12 weeks); second trimester abortions are much riskier. Disturbing trends: illegal MTPs by unqualified quacks and misuse of amniocentesis for sex determination followed by MTP of female foetuses — totally against the law.

3.4 Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)

Also called venereal diseases (VD) or reproductive tract infections (RTI).

Common STIsCurable?
Gonorrhoea, syphilis, chlamydiasis, genital warts, trichomoniasisCompletely curable if detected early and treated properly
Hepatitis-B, genital herpes, HIV (AIDS)Not completely curable
  • HIV infection is the most dangerous. Hepatitis-B and HIV also spread by sharing injection needles or surgical instruments, blood transfusion, and from infected mother to foetus.
  • Early symptoms are minor: itching, fluid discharge, slight pain, swellings in the genital region. Infected females are often asymptomatic.
  • Late complications: pelvic inflammatory diseases (PID), abortions, still births, ectopic pregnancies, infertility or even cancer of the reproductive tract.
  • Highest risk group: 15–24 years.
Prevention(i) Avoid sex with unknown / multiple partners; (ii) always use condoms; (iii) in case of doubt, see a qualified doctor for early detection and complete treatment.
Memory trickIncurable STIs: “H-H-H” → Hepatitis-B, genital Herpes, HIV.

3.5 Infertility

  • Inability to conceive even after 2 years of unprotected sexual cohabitation. Causes: physical, congenital, drugs, immunological or psychological.
  • In India the female is often blamed, but more often than not the problem lies in the male partner.

Assisted reproductive technologies (ART)

TechniqueWhat happens (NCERT)
IVF–ET (test tube baby)In vitro fertilisation (outside the body, in simulated lab conditions) followed by embryo transfer; ova from wife/donor, sperms from husband/donor
ZIFTZygote intra fallopian transfer: zygote or early embryo with up to 8 blastomeres into the fallopian tube
IUTIntra uterine transfer: embryo with more than 8 blastomeres into the uterus
In-vivo fertilisation + transferEmbryos formed by fusion of gametes within a female can also be transferred
GIFTGamete intra fallopian transfer: ovum from a donor into the fallopian tube of a female who cannot produce one but can provide a suitable environment
ICSIIntra cytoplasmic sperm injection: sperm directly injected into the ovum in the laboratory
AI / IUIArtificial insemination for males unable to inseminate or with very low sperm counts: semen from husband or donor introduced into the vagina or uterus (IUI — intra-uterine insemination)
TrapZIFT = Zygote to the Fallopian tube (≤ 8 cells); IUT = embryo > 8 cells to the uterus; GIFT transfers a gamete, not a zygote. ICSI = sperm injected into ovum.
  • ART is available in very few centres and is expensive; emotional, religious and social factors also deter. Legal adoption is one of the best methods for couples seeking parenthood.
